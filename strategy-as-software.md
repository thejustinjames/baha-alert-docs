# Strategy as Software — Seed Note

## Core Idea

There may be a product opportunity around the concept of **Strategy as Software**.

The underlying thesis is that strategy is shifting from something that is periodically written, presented, and reviewed into something that can increasingly be encoded into systems.

Instead of:

> Strategy → PowerPoint → meetings → interpretation → execution

the emerging model may become:

> Strategy → rules, constraints, signals, decisions → continuous execution and learning

In other words, strategy becomes partially executable.

## The Opportunity

Most organisations still treat strategy as a static artefact:

- annual plans
- board decks
- OKRs
- consultancy reports
- transformation roadmaps
- operating models
- governance documents

The problem is that there is usually a large gap between what the organisation says its strategy is and what its systems, incentives, people, and processes actually do.

A Strategy as Software product could attempt to close that gap.

The system would translate strategic intent into an operational decision layer that can continuously:

1. **Sense** — observe relevant internal and external signals.
2. **Interpret** — understand those signals against strategic priorities.
3. **Decide** — recommend or make decisions within agreed boundaries.
4. **Act** — trigger workflows, actions, escalations, or resource allocation.
5. **Measure** — determine whether actions are producing the intended outcomes.
6. **Learn** — adjust recommendations or surface where the strategy itself may need revision.

## Possible Product Question

Can an organisation turn its strategy into a living operating system rather than a document?

This does not necessarily mean allowing AI to autonomously run a company.

The more credible proposition may be:

> Encode the organisation's strategic intent, decision rules, constraints, priorities, risk tolerances and desired outcomes into a governed software layer that helps management make consistent decisions.

## Potential Components

A future product might include:

### Strategic Intent Model

Capture:

- objectives
- priorities
- trade-offs
- non-negotiables
- risk appetite
- investment principles
- customer priorities
- market assumptions

### Decision Engine

Evaluate decisions against the strategy.

Example:

> "Should we enter this market?"

The system would assess the proposal against strategic objectives, capital constraints, market criteria, risk thresholds and previous decisions.

### Strategy Contradiction Detection

Identify when actions conflict with declared strategy.

Examples:

- claiming customer experience is the priority while reducing frontline capacity;
- claiming cost discipline while approving low-return projects;
- claiming AI-first while preserving workflows that prevent automation;
- claiming a focus on core markets while continually funding peripheral expansion.

### Strategic Memory

Maintain the reasoning behind previous decisions so that the organisation does not repeatedly revisit the same arguments without understanding why earlier decisions were made.

### Signal Fabric

Continuously ingest relevant business signals and identify when assumptions underlying the strategy may no longer hold.

### Governance Layer

Define:

- what AI can recommend;
- what it can execute;
- what requires human approval;
- escalation thresholds;
- decision ownership;
- audit trail;
- blast radius.

### Strategy Simulator

Allow leadership to test:

> "If we change this assumption, priority or constraint, what downstream decisions change?"

## The Changing Role of the Adviser

One of the potentially important consequences of Strategy as Software is that it may change what strategic advisers actually do.

The key question is:

> **Does the adviser move from producing the strategy to designing the rules, constraints and judgement system within which the strategy continuously operates?**

If so, the value of the adviser shifts away from producing a periodic strategy document and towards helping define:

- decision principles;
- strategic constraints;
- acceptable trade-offs;
- risk boundaries;
- escalation rules;
- judgement criteria;
- governance mechanisms;
- the assumptions that should trigger strategic reconsideration.

This could represent a meaningful evolution of management consulting and board advisory: from strategy production to strategy system design.

## Potential Users

Initial users could include:

- CEOs
- boards
- corporate strategy teams
- private equity operating partners
- family offices
- transformation offices
- business-unit leaders
- management consultancies
- investment committees

## Interesting Positioning

This should probably not be positioned as:

> "AI that writes your strategy."

That market is likely to become commoditised.

The more interesting proposition is:

> **Software that helps organisations execute strategy consistently.**

Or:

> **Turn strategy from a document into a decision system.**

## Relationship to Existing Thinking

This idea potentially intersects with several existing areas:

- AI governance
- autonomous agents
- decision intelligence
- organisational operating models
- Strategy as Code
- policy engines
- enterprise architecture
- OKRs
- management consulting
- scenario planning
- digital twins
- Signal Fabric
- governed autonomous systems

It may also connect naturally with the broader thesis that as AI becomes capable of taking actions rather than merely producing information, organisations will need a mechanism for defining what the organisation actually wants the machine to optimise for.

That may ultimately be the strategy layer.

## Key Question to Explore Later

The fundamental product question may be:

> **What is the minimum representation of a company's strategy that is precise enough for software to reason against it, but flexible enough to reflect the ambiguity of real business strategy?**

That is probably where the interesting intellectual and technical problem begins.
